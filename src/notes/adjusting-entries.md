## 1 · Types of Entries

Before solving adjustments, distinguish these three:

### 1. Reclassifying entry

Used when the account title/classification is wrong, but the amount and basic transaction are otherwise properly recorded.

- **Keyword:** CLASSIFICATION — the error is in which account was used.
- **Purpose:** Transfer the amount from the wrong account to the correct account.

### 2. Correcting entry

Used to correct other accounting errors, such as:

- wrong amount
- omitted account
- wrong side of the entry
- incorrect recording of a transaction
- other errors aside from simple misclassification

**Purpose:** Correct the accounting error.

### 3. Adjusting entry

Used at the end of an accounting period to update account balances for economic events that have already occurred but have not yet been completely recorded.

> **Memory phrase: ADJUST BEFORE YOU REPORT (ABR)**
> Adjusting entries ensure that revenues and expenses are reported in the proper accounting period under the accrual basis of accounting.

## 2 · Why Are Adjusting Entries Needed?

Businesses continuously earn revenue and incur expenses. However, some economic events are not recorded immediately because:

- cash has not yet been received;
- cash has not yet been paid;
- a prepaid amount has already been partly consumed; or
- an advance collection has already been partly earned.

Therefore, adjusting entries make the accounting records reflect what actually happened during the period, not merely what was paid or received.

> **Key rule**
> Record what happened, not just what was paid or received.

Adjusting entries help ensure that:

- Revenues are recognized when earned.
- Expenses are recognized when incurred.
- Assets and liabilities are properly stated at the reporting date.
- Financial statements present accurate information.

## 3 · Recognition and Derecognition

### Recognition principle

**Recognition** is the process of including an item in the financial statements when it meets the definition and recognition criteria of an:

- Asset
- Liability
- Income
- Expense
- Equity

#### Recognition of income

Income is generally recognized when it results in:

<p class="note-formula">Increase in Asset OR Decrease in Liability</p>

provided that the transaction is not an investment from owners.

#### Recognition of expense

Expense is generally recognized when it results in:

<p class="note-formula">Decrease in Asset OR Increase in Liability</p>

provided that the transaction is not a withdrawal/distribution to owners.

### Derecognition principle

**Derecognition** is the process of removing an asset or liability from the financial statements when it no longer meets the applicable recognition criteria.

## 4 · Two Major Categories of Adjusting Entries

Adjusting entries are generally classified into:

| Category | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| **A. Accruals** | Something happened first → recording comes later |
| **B. Deferrals** | Cash/payment happens first → recognition comes later |

## 5 · Adjusting Entries for Accruals

### What is an accrual?

An **accrual** means recognizing revenue when it is earned and recognizing expense when it is incurred, regardless of when cash is received or paid.

<p class="note-formula">ACCRUAL = ACTION FIRST, CASH LATER</p>

### 1. Accrued salaries / accrued expense

Salaries are usually paid at regular intervals. Sometimes employees have already worked before the end of the accounting period, but payday has not yet arrived. The expense has already been incurred, so it must be recognized.

**Pro forma entry**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
| --- | :---: | :---: |
| Salaries Expense | ✓ | |
| &emsp;Salaries Payable | | ✓ |

**Pattern:** Expense ↑ → Liability ↑

**Example** — ABC Company has 2 employees, each earning ₱1,000 per day. Employees are paid every 10th and 25th day of the month. At December 31, the employees have not yet been paid for December 26–31.

**Computation**

- ₱1,000/day × 6 unpaid days = ₱6,000 per employee
- ₱6,000 × 2 employees = **₱12,000**

**Adjusting entry**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Salaries Expense | ₱12,000 | |
| &emsp;Salaries Payable | | ₱12,000 |

**Why?** The employees already worked from December 26–31. Therefore Salaries Expense must increase, and Salaries Payable must increase because the amount is still unpaid.

### 2. Accrued interest expense

Interest expense is recognized as it is incurred, even if payment will happen later.

<p class="note-formula">Interest = Principal × Rate × Time</p>

- **P** = Principal
- **R** = Interest rate
- **T** = Time

Since interest rates are normally annual, Time must be expressed as a fraction of a year.

**Example** — On December 1, an entity borrowed ₱100,000 at 12% annual interest, payable after one year. Prepare the adjusting entry on December 31.

**Computation:** Interest = ₱100,000 × 12% × 1/12 = **₱1,000**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Interest Expense | ₱1,000 | |
| &emsp;Interest Payable | | ₱1,000 |

**Pattern:** Expense ↑ → Liability ↑

### 3. Accrued uncollectible accounts

Businesses may sell goods or provide services on credit. Some receivables may eventually become uncollectible. Because the exact amount may not yet be known, the entity may estimate it based on experience or an appropriate estimation method.

**Common simplified method:** Percentage of Credit Sales

**Pro forma entry**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
| --- | :---: | :---: |
| Doubtful Accounts Expense | ✓ | |
| &emsp;Allowance for Doubtful Accounts | | ✓ |

**Example** — Credit sales = ₱1,000,000 · Estimated uncollectible rate = 2%

**Computation:** ₱1,000,000 × 2% = **₱20,000**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Doubtful Accounts Expense | ₱20,000 | |
| &emsp;Allowance for Doubtful Accounts | | ₱20,000 |

**Why an allowance instead of directly reducing Accounts Receivable?** Because the ₱20,000 is only an estimate. The allowance is a contra-asset account presented against Accounts Receivable.

#### Contra accounts

A **contra account** is paired with another account and has a normal balance opposite to the associated account.

| Associated account | Contra account | Normal balance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Asset | Contra Asset | Credit |
| Liability | Contra Liability | Debit |
| Equity | Contra Equity | Debit |
| Revenue/Income | Contra Revenue | Debit |
| Expense | Contra Expense | Credit |

**Most important example:** Accounts Receivable → debit normal balance · Allowance for Doubtful Accounts → credit normal balance

<p class="note-formula">CONTRA = OPPOSITE NORMAL BALANCE</p>

### 4. Accrued interest income / accrued revenue

This occurs when the entity has already earned income, but cash has not yet been received.

**Pro forma entry**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
| --- | :---: | :---: |
| Interest Receivable | ✓ | |
| &emsp;Interest Income | | ✓ |

**Pattern:** Asset ↑ → Income ↑

**Example** — An entity has earned ₱5,000 interest by December 31, but the customer will pay next year.

| Account | Debit | Credit |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Interest Receivable | ₱5,000 | |
| &emsp;Interest Income | | ₱5,000 |

**Why?** The income has already been earned, so it must be recognized in the current period. Because cash has not yet been received, a receivable is recognized.

### Accruals — quick pattern

| Type | Debit | Credit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Accrued Expense | Expense | Payable |
| Accrued Interest Expense | Interest Expense | Interest Payable |
| Accrued Revenue | Receivable | Income |
| Accrued Interest Income | Interest Receivable | Interest Income |
| Uncollectible Accounts | Doubtful Accounts Expense | Allowance for Doubtful Accounts |

> **Golden rule**
> Accruals increase BOTH an Income Statement account and a Balance Sheet account.

## 6 · Deferrals — Prepaid Expenses

### What is a deferral?

A **deferral** occurs when cash is paid or received before the related expense or income is recognized. Two common deferrals:

1. Prepaid Expense / Deferred Expense
2. Advance Collection / Deferred Revenue

<p class="note-formula">DEFERRAL = CASH FIRST, RECOGNITION LATER</p>

### Prepaid expenses

A **prepaid expense** occurs when an entity pays for a benefit before it is consumed.

- Prepaid insurance
- Prepaid rent
- Prepaid subscriptions

Initially, the amount may be recorded as an asset because the future benefit has not yet been used.

#### Two methods

**A. Asset method** — the entire payment is initially recorded as an asset.

- Initial entry: Debit Prepaid Expense / Credit Cash
- At adjustment: Debit Expense / Credit Prepaid Expense

<p class="note-formula">ASSET METHOD → ADJUSTMENT DEBITS EXPENSE</p>

**B. Expense method** — the entire payment is initially recorded as an expense.

- Initial entry: Debit Expense / Credit Cash
- At adjustment: Debit Prepaid Expense / Credit Expense

<p class="note-formula">EXPENSE METHOD → ADJUSTMENT DEBITS ASSET</p>

> **Important**
> Both methods can result in the same final balances after adjustment. If the problem does not specify the method, your course/instructor may require the Asset Method, so follow the convention used in your class.

#### Prepaid expense — the 3 important dates

Whenever you encounter a prepaid expense problem, identify:

1. **Transaction date** — when payment was made.
2. **Adjustment date** — usually the end of the reporting period.
3. **Expiration date** — when the prepaid benefit completely expires.

<p class="note-formula">Transaction Date → Adjustment Date → Expiration Date</p>

| Period | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Transaction date → expiration date | **Total life** |
| Transaction date → adjustment date | **Expired portion = Expense** |
| Adjustment date → expiration date | **Unexpired portion = Asset** |

<p class="note-formula">EXPIRED = EXPENSE &nbsp;·&nbsp; UNEXPIRED = ASSET</p>

#### Prepaid insurance example

ABC Company paid ₱600,000 on May 31, 2020 for one-year insurance coverage from May 31, 2020 to May 31, 2021. Reporting date: December 31, 2020.

1. **Total life:** May 31, 2020 → May 31, 2021 = 12 months
2. **Expired portion:** May 31 → December 31 = 7 months
3. **Unexpired portion:** December 31 → May 31 = 5 months
4. **Monthly expense:** ₱600,000 ÷ 12 months = ₱50,000/month

- Expired expense = ₱50,000 × 7 months = **₱350,000**
- Unexpired asset = ₱50,000 × 5 months = **₱250,000**

**Asset method**

*May 31 — initial entry*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Prepaid Insurance | ₱600,000 | |
| &emsp;Cash | | ₱600,000 |

*December 31 — adjusting entry*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Insurance Expense | ₱350,000 | |
| &emsp;Prepaid Insurance | | ₱350,000 |

**Ending balances:** Cash decreases ₱600,000 · Prepaid Insurance = ₱600,000 − ₱350,000 = ₱250,000 · Insurance Expense = ₱350,000

**Expense method**

*May 31 — initial entry*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Insurance Expense | ₱600,000 | |
| &emsp;Cash | | ₱600,000 |

At December 31, only ₱350,000 should remain as expense. Therefore, the excess ₱250,000 must be transferred back to an asset.

*December 31 — adjusting entry*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Prepaid Insurance | ₱250,000 | |
| &emsp;Insurance Expense | | ₱250,000 |

**Ending balances:** Insurance Expense = ₱600,000 − ₱250,000 = ₱350,000 · Prepaid Insurance = ₱250,000

#### Asset vs. expense method

| | Asset Method | Expense Method |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Initial debit** | Prepaid Expense | Expense |
| **Initial credit** | Cash | Cash |
| **Adjustment debit** | Expense | Prepaid Expense |
| **Adjustment credit** | Prepaid Expense | Expense |
| **Final expense** | Same | Same |
| **Final asset** | Same | Same |

> **Memory trick**
> Asset → Expense · Expense → Asset. After the first adjusting entry, the Expense Method effectively reaches the same balances as the Asset Method.

## 7 · Deferrals — Advance Collection

An **advance collection** occurs when a customer pays before the entity has earned the income.

- Advance rent
- Subscription received in advance
- Customer deposits for future services

The amount is initially a liability because the entity still owes the customer goods or services.

### Two methods

**A. Liability method** — the entire collection is initially recorded as a liability.

- Initial entry: Debit Cash / Credit Unearned Revenue
- At adjustment: Debit Unearned Revenue / Credit Revenue/Income

<p class="note-formula">LIABILITY METHOD → ADJUSTMENT CREDITS INCOME</p>

**B. Income method** — the entire collection is initially recorded as income.

- Initial entry: Debit Cash / Credit Revenue/Income
- At adjustment: Debit Revenue/Income / Credit Unearned Revenue

<p class="note-formula">INCOME METHOD → ADJUSTMENT CREDITS LIABILITY</p>

### Advance collection — the 3 important dates

1. **Transaction date** — when the cash was received.
2. **Adjustment date** — end of the reporting period.
3. **Expiration date** — end of the period covered by the collection.

<p class="note-formula">Transaction Date → Adjustment Date → Expiration Date</p>

| Period | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Transaction date → expiration date | **Total life** |
| Transaction date → adjustment date | **Expired portion = Income earned** |
| Adjustment date → expiration date | **Unexpired portion = Liability** |

<p class="note-formula">EXPIRED = INCOME &nbsp;·&nbsp; UNEXPIRED = LIABILITY</p>

### Advance rent example

An entity received ₱1,200,000 on October 1, 2021 for one year of rent covering October 1, 2021 to October 1, 2022. Reporting date: December 31, 2021.

- Total life = 12 months
- Expired portion: October → December = 3 months
- Unexpired portion: January → October = 9 months
- Monthly rent = ₱1,200,000 ÷ 12 = ₱100,000/month
- Earned income = ₱100,000 × 3 months = **₱300,000**
- Unearned liability = ₱100,000 × 9 months = **₱900,000**

**Liability method**

*October 1 — initial entry*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Cash | ₱1,200,000 | |
| &emsp;Unearned Rent Income | | ₱1,200,000 |

*December 31 — adjusting entry*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Unearned Rent Income | ₱300,000 | |
| &emsp;Rent Income | | ₱300,000 |

**Ending balances:** Cash = ₱1,200,000 · Unearned Rent Income = ₱1,200,000 − ₱300,000 = ₱900,000 · Rent Income = ₱300,000

**Income method**

*October 1 — initial entry*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Cash | ₱1,200,000 | |
| &emsp;Rent Income | | ₱1,200,000 |

But only ₱300,000 has actually been earned. Therefore, ₱900,000 must be transferred from income to a liability.

*December 31 — adjusting entry*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Rent Income | ₱900,000 | |
| &emsp;Unearned Rent Income | | ₱900,000 |

**Ending balances:** Rent Income = ₱1,200,000 − ₱900,000 = ₱300,000 · Unearned Rent Income = ₱900,000

#### Liability vs. income method

| | Liability Method | Income Method |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Initial debit** | Cash | Cash |
| **Initial credit** | Unearned Revenue | Revenue |
| **Adjustment debit** | Unearned Revenue | Revenue |
| **Adjustment credit** | Revenue | Unearned Revenue |
| **Final income** | Same | Same |
| **Final liability** | Same | Same |

> **Memory trick**
> Liability → Income · Income → Liability. After the first adjusting entry, the Income Method reaches the same balances as the Liability Method.

## 8 · Depreciation

**Depreciation** is the systematic allocation of the depreciable amount of a long-lived asset over its useful life.

Examples of depreciable assets: buildings, equipment, vehicles, computers, office furniture.

> **Important**
> Depreciation is not the process of determining the market value of an asset. It is the allocation of the asset's depreciable cost over its useful life.

### Four important depreciation factors

1. **Cost** — amount incurred to acquire the asset.
2. **Estimated useful life** — estimated period the asset will be used.
3. **Salvage value** — estimated amount that can be recovered at the end of the asset's useful life.
4. **Depreciable cost** — the amount subject to depreciation.

<p class="note-formula">Depreciable Cost = Cost − Salvage Value</p>

### Straight-line method

<p class="note-formula">Depreciation Expense per Year = (Cost − Salvage Value) ÷ Useful Life</p>

### Accumulated depreciation

**Accumulated Depreciation** is the total depreciation recognized from the time the asset was placed in service up to the reporting date. It is a **contra-asset account**, and therefore has a credit normal balance.

<p class="note-formula">Accumulated Depreciation = Total Depreciation Recorded to Date</p>

### Carrying amount / book value

The **carrying amount** is the amount at which the asset is reported after accumulated depreciation.

<p class="note-formula">Cost − Accumulated Depreciation = Carrying Amount / Book Value</p>

### Depreciation journal entry

**Pro forma entry**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
| --- | :---: | :---: |
| Depreciation Expense | ✓ | |
| &emsp;Accumulated Depreciation | | ✓ |

**Pattern:** Expense ↑ → Contra-Asset ↑. Because Accumulated Depreciation has a credit balance, increasing it reduces the asset's carrying amount.

### Depreciation example

ABC Company purchased equipment for ₱6,000,000 on May 31, 2021. Useful life = 5 years. Salvage value = ₱1,000,000.

1. **Depreciable cost:** ₱6,000,000 − ₱1,000,000 = ₱5,000,000
2. **Annual depreciation:** ₱5,000,000 ÷ 5 years = ₱1,000,000 per year
3. **2021 depreciation:** acquired May 31; May 31 to December 31 = 7 months → ₱1,000,000 × 7/12 = ₱583,333.33

*Adjusting entry — December 31, 2021*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Depreciation Expense | ₱583,333.33 | |
| &emsp;Accumulated Depreciation | | ₱583,333.33 |

Carrying amount = ₱6,000,000 − ₱583,333.33 = **₱5,416,666.67**

#### Depreciation in subsequent years

For 2022, assuming the equipment was used for the entire year, annual depreciation = ₱1,000,000.

| Account | Debit | Credit |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Depreciation Expense | ₱1,000,000 | |
| &emsp;Accumulated Depreciation | | ₱1,000,000 |

- Accumulated depreciation after 2022 = ₱583,333.33 + ₱1,000,000 = **₱1,583,333.33**
- Carrying amount = ₱6,000,000 − ₱1,583,333.33 = **₱4,416,666.67**

> **Important: first and last year**
> When an asset is purchased or placed in service in the middle of the year, the first year's depreciation may be partial-year depreciation. Likewise, the final year may contain only a partial year's depreciation.
>
> Always check: When did the asset start being used? What reporting period is being asked? How many months did the asset provide service during that period?

## 9 · The Big Picture

### Accruals

- **Accrued expense** — something was used/incurred, but payment has not yet been made.
  - Expense ↑ + Liability ↑
  - Examples: Salaries Expense, Interest Expense
- **Accrued revenue** — something was earned, but cash has not yet been received.
  - Asset ↑ + Income ↑
  - Examples: Accounts/Interest Receivable, Revenue/Interest Income

### Deferrals

- **Prepaid expense** — cash was paid first, the benefit is consumed later. Initial: Asset.
  - Adjustment: Expense ↑ + Asset ↓
  - Examples: Insurance Expense, Prepaid Insurance
- **Advance collection** — cash was received first, revenue is earned later. Initial: Liability.
  - Adjustment: Liability ↓ + Income ↑
  - Examples: Unearned Revenue, Revenue

## 10 · Master Table — Adjusting Entries

| Situation | What happened? | Debit | Credit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Accrued Expense | Expense incurred, unpaid | Expense | Payable |
| Accrued Revenue | Revenue earned, unreceived | Receivable | Revenue |
| Uncollectible Accounts | Receivable estimated uncollectible | Doubtful Accounts Expense | Allowance |
| Prepaid Expense — Asset Method | Benefit already consumed | Expense | Prepaid Asset |
| Prepaid Expense — Expense Method | Unused benefit remains | Prepaid Asset | Expense |
| Advance Collection — Liability Method | Revenue already earned | Unearned Revenue | Revenue |
| Advance Collection — Income Method | Unearned portion remains | Revenue | Unearned Revenue |
| Depreciation | Asset used during period | Depreciation Expense | Accumulated Depreciation |

## 11 · The Fastest Way to Solve Adjusting Entries

When you see an adjustment problem, follow these steps:

1. **Identify the type**
   - Did it happen but wasn't recorded yet? → **ACCRUAL**
   - Was cash paid/received first, and recognition comes later? → **DEFERRAL**
2. **Identify the accounts affected** — is it an expense? income? an asset? a liability? a contra-asset?
3. **Compute the amount** — look for unpaid days, months, interest rate, principal, percentage, useful life, salvage value, expired period, unexpired period.
4. **Determine the direction** — what needs to increase? What needs to decrease? Then apply the debit and credit rules.
5. **Write the adjusting entry.**

> **Remember**
> Every adjusting entry affects at least ONE Income Statement account and ONE Balance Sheet account.

## 12 · Must-Memorize Patterns & Memory Tricks

### Accruals

- **Accrued expense** — DR Expense / CR Payable
- **Accrued revenue** — DR Receivable / CR Income
- **Uncollectible accounts** — DR Doubtful Accounts Expense / CR Allowance for Doubtful Accounts

### Deferrals

- **Prepaid expense — Asset method:** DR Expense / CR Prepaid Asset
- **Prepaid expense — Expense method:** DR Prepaid Asset / CR Expense
- **Advance collection — Liability method:** DR Unearned Revenue / CR Revenue
- **Advance collection — Income method:** DR Revenue / CR Unearned Revenue

### Depreciation

- DR Depreciation Expense / CR Accumulated Depreciation

### High-yield memory tricks

1. **Accrual** — ACTION FIRST, CASH LATER. Something already happened, but cash/recording comes later.
2. **Prepaid expense** — EXPIRED = EXPENSE, UNEXPIRED = ASSET
3. **Advance collection** — EXPIRED/EARNED = INCOME, UNEXPIRED/UNEARNED = LIABILITY
4. **Contra** — CONTRA = OPPOSITE NORMAL BALANCE
5. **Depreciation** — COST − SALVAGE = DEPRECIABLE COST, then DEPRECIABLE COST ÷ USEFUL LIFE = ANNUAL DEPRECIATION
6. **Book value** — COST − ACCUMULATED DEPRECIATION = CARRYING AMOUNT

## 13 · Common Traps to Avoid

> **❌ Trap #1: Cash is always involved in an adjusting entry.**
> False. Adjusting entries generally do not involve Cash. If cash is being received or paid as part of the entry, you may actually be looking at the original transaction or a subsequent payment/collection rather than the adjusting entry.

> **❌ Trap #2: Prepaid means expense immediately.**
> Not necessarily. Under the Asset Method, a prepaid expense is initially an asset. Only the expired/used portion becomes expense.

> **❌ Trap #3: All cash received is automatically income.**
> False. Cash received in advance may initially be a liability. Only the earned portion is income.

> **❌ Trap #4: Depreciation directly credits Equipment.**
> Usually, the adjusting entry uses Accumulated Depreciation, not the Equipment account itself. This keeps the original cost of the asset visible while accumulated depreciation is presented as a contra-asset.

> **❌ Trap #5: Accrued revenue means cash was already received.**
> No. Accrued revenue means revenue has already been earned, but cash has not yet been received.

## 14 · One-Page Cheat Sheet

> **Ask yourself: "HAS IT HAPPENED?"**
> YES, but NOT RECORDED? → **ACCRUAL**
> NO, because CASH HAPPENED FIRST? → **DEFERRAL**

- **Accrual**
  - Expense: DR Expense / CR Payable
  - Revenue: DR Receivable / CR Revenue
- **Prepaid expense**
  - Expired portion → Expense
  - Unexpired portion → Asset
- **Advance collection**
  - Earned portion → Income
  - Unearned portion → Liability
- **Depreciation**
  - Cost − Salvage Value = Depreciable Cost
  - Depreciable Cost ÷ Useful Life = Annual Depreciation
  - DR Depreciation Expense / CR Accumulated Depreciation

### Final memory map

<p class="note-formula">ACCRUAL = HAPPENED FIRST &nbsp;·&nbsp; DEFERRAL = CASH FIRST</p>

| Situation | Pattern |
| --- | --- |
| Accrued Expense | Expense → Payable |
| Accrued Revenue | Receivable → Income |
| Prepaid Expense | Asset → Expense |
| Advance Collection | Liability → Income |
| Depreciation | Expense → Contra-Asset |
| Contra | Opposite Normal Balance |
| Prepaid | Expired = Expense · Unexpired = Asset |
| Advance Collection | Earned = Income · Unearned = Liability |

> **Financial reporting rule**
> Recognize revenues when earned and expenses when incurred. **ADJUST BEFORE YOU REPORT.**
