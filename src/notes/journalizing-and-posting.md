## 1 · What is Journalizing?

**Journalizing** is the process of recording transactions in the journal in chronological order.

A **Journal** is a chronological record of the entity's transactions. It is also known as the **Book of Original Entry**.

### Two types of journal

1. **Special Journal** — used to record repetitive, day-to-day business transactions. Instead of mixing everything together, businesses group similar transactions to keep things organized and easy to track.
   - Ex. Sales Journal, Purchases Journal, Cash Receipts Journal, Cash Payments/Disbursements Journal
2. **General Journal** — used to record any unique, unusual, or uncommon business transactions that do not fit into the special journals.
   - Ex. Adjusting Entries, Correcting Errors, or Buying Fixed Assets on credit

## 2 · Purpose of Journalizing & Parts of a Journal Entry

Journalizing creates a complete record of:

- What happened
- When it happened
- Which accounts were affected

> **Memory tip**
> Think of the journal as the company's diary.

### Parts of a journal entry

1. Date
2. Account Title
3. Debit Amount
4. Credit Amount
5. Explanation

## 3 · Debit on the Left, Credit on the Right

Accounting follows one simple layout:

- **Debit = Left**
- **Credit = Right**

**Example** — Nov. 25, 2025

| Account | Debit | Credit |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Equipment | ₱8,000 | |
| &emsp;Accounts Payable | | ₱8,000 |

*To record the purchase of a water dispenser by Surume's Coffee Shop on account.*

> **Mnemonic: DLRC**
> **D**ebit **L**eft — **R**ight **C**redit

## 4 · Double-Entry Accounting

Every journal entry must satisfy:

<p class="note-formula">Total Debits = Total Credits</p>

This keeps records balanced.

### Simple journal entry

A transaction affecting only two accounts.

**Example** — Santivag Delivery Services received ₱20,000 for its services.

| Account | Debit | Credit |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Cash | ₱20,000 | |
| &emsp;Service Revenue | | ₱20,000 |

*To record payment for Santivag Services.*

### Compound journal entry

A transaction affecting three or more accounts.

**Example** — Surume's Coffee Shop purchases commercial coffee grinding equipment for ₱100,000, paying ₱30,000 in cash upfront and placing the remaining ₱70,000 on account.

| Account | Debit | Credit |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Equipment | ₱100,000 | |
| &emsp;Cash | | ₱30,000 |
| &emsp;Accounts Payable | | ₱70,000 |

*To record the purchase of commercial equipment paid partially in cash with the balance on account.*

## 5 · Journalizing Procedure & Common Errors

### Journalizing procedure

1. Analyze the transaction
2. Determine the accounts
3. Identify the debit and credit
4. Record the entry
5. Verify equality

> **Mnemonic: ADIRV**
> **A**nalyze, **D**etermine, **I**dentify, **R**ecord, **V**erify.

### Common journalizing errors

- Wrong account title
- Reversed debit and credit
- Unequal entries
- Omitted transactions

> **Memory tip**
> Always review before posting.

## 6 · What is Posting?

**Posting** is the transferring of amounts from the journal to the appropriate accounts in the ledger.

A **Ledger** is the book used to compute the balance of each account title. It is also known as the **Book of Final Entry** / **Book of Secondary Entry**.

### Two types of ledger

1. **General Ledger** — the main accounting record that holds a summary of every account in the business. It gives you the big picture of your entire financial status.
2. **Subsidiary Ledger** — a detailed sub-record that breaks down a specific summary account from the General Ledger. It provides the exact individual details, like each customer's balance, to support the total figure.

### General ledger vs. subsidiary ledger

| Category | General Ledger | Subsidiary Ledger |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Nature** | Group of accounts with different characteristics | Group of accounts with similar characteristics |
| **Use** | Required in recording the accounts | Optional in the recording process |
| **Link to Trial Balance** | The Trial Balance is made using the General Ledger | The Trial Balance is not prepared using the Subsidiary Ledger |
| **Relationship** | The General Ledger controls the Subsidiary Ledger | The Subsidiary Ledger is part of the General Ledger |
| **In case of conflict** | The General Ledger follows the amount in the Subsidiary Ledger | The amount computed in the Subsidiary Ledger is used |

## 7 · Steps in Posting

1. Prepare each of the account titles in the Chart of Accounts.
2. Transfer the journal entry to the ledger according to its Posting Reference.
3. Total the debits and credits — if debits are higher, the account has a debit balance; if credits are higher, it has a credit balance.
4. Use the computed amounts to make the Trial Balance.

## 8 · Trial Balance

> **Trial Balance**
> A list of all accounts with their respective debit or credit balances. It is prepared to verify the equality of debits and credits, and should list the account titles in numerical order.

### Locating errors if the trial balance is unequal

- Error in posting a transaction in the Ledger
- Error in determining account balances
- Error in preparing the Trial Balance

### Discrepancy clues in miscalculation

- **Transposition** — suggested when the discrepancy is divisible by 9. Reversing the order of numbers.
- **Slide** — moving a decimal point.

### Errors not detected by an equal trial balance

> **Mnemonic: FSWAmWAc**
> **F**ailure, **S**ame, **W**rong **Am**ount, **W**rong **Ac**count

1. **Failure** to record or post a transaction
2. Recording the **Same** transaction more than once
3. Recording **Wrong amounts** in both the debit and credit
4. Posting to the **Wrong account** with the same debit and credit amount
