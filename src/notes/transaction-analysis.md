## 1 · Transaction Analysis

### What is transaction analysis?

- **Transaction Analysis** is the first step in the accounting cycle. It involves examining business events to determine whether they affect the accounting records.
  - **Key question:** Did this event impact the company's assets, liabilities, or owner's equity?
  - **Memory tip:** Think like a detective before becoming a recorder.

### Accountable event vs. non-accountable event

- **Accountable event**
  - Transactions that will be recorded in the Journal.
- **Non-accountable event**
  - Transactions that are not recorded in the Journal.
    - **Ignored**
      - if not required by a standard to be disclosed
      - if the problem is *silent*, just ignore the non-accountable event
    - **Disclosed**
      - if it is required by the standard to be disclosed
      - Example: related party transactions

> **Note**
> All accountable events are required to be recorded in the Journal. A non-accountable event is not necessarily ignored — some are required to be disclosed in the Notes to Financial Statements by the Accounting Standard.

### How do you analyze a transaction?

The big question is how you will analyze a transaction. Let us use two simple words to explain it.

- **Classifying** — knowing the account titles that are affected by the transaction.
  - Two or more accounts must be affected because we are using the **Double-Entry System** in recording every transaction.
- **Measuring** — knowing the monetary value of the transaction.

> **Note**
> Classification and Measurement must be both present in order for a transaction to be labeled as an accountable event.

Every transaction must satisfy:

<p class="note-formula">Assets = Liabilities + Owner's Equity</p>

This equation is the foundation of accounting and must always remain balanced.

- **Mnemonic: ALE**
  - **A** = Assets (what you own)
  - **L** = Liabilities (what you owe)
  - **E** = Equity (net value)
- Use the **AID Method** when analyzing transactions:
  - **A** = Accounts affected
  - **I** = Increase or decrease?
  - **D** = Debit or credit?

## 2 · Normal Balances

Accounts increase either through debits or credits.

| Normal balance | Accounts |
| --- | --- |
| **Debit** | Drawing / Withdrawals, Expenses, Assets |
| **Credit** | Liabilities, Equity, Revenue |

> **Mnemonic: DEA-LER**
> **D**rawing, **E**xpenses, **A**ssets are increased by debits — **L**iabilities, **E**quity, **R**evenue are increased by credits.

- **Asset exchange transactions**
  - Sometimes one asset increases while another decreases.
    - Example: Lukas purchased equipment for cash.
      - Equipment increases
      - Cash decreases
      - No effect on total assets
- **Source documents**
  - Transaction analysis starts with source documents such as:
    - Receipts
    - Invoices
    - Deposit slips
    - Checks
    - Contracts
      - *Remember: No source document, no reliable transaction.*
- **Business vs. personal transactions**
  - Only business transactions are recorded.
  - Example:
    - Business transaction: Nayeom paid the electricity bill.
    - Non-business transaction: Nayeom bought personal groceries.
      - This follows the **Business Entity Principle**.
- **Revenue transactions**
  - Revenue increases owner's equity.
  - Example:
    - Swift Go Delivery Services performed services worth ₱10,000 and received cash.
      - Cash increases
      - Service Revenue increases
- **Expense transactions**
  - Expenses decrease owner's equity.
  - Example:
    - Sue Plyes paid salaries to its employees.
      - Salaries Expense increases
      - Cash decreases
- **Transaction analysis golden rule**
  - Every valid business transaction:
    - affects at least two accounts,
    - maintains the accounting equation, and
    - can be supported by source documents.
      - *Memory phrase: Analyze before you record.*

## Summary

### Transaction analysis (RAID-NBS)

1. **R**ecordable or not recordable — Accounting Event or Economic Business Transaction
2. **A**ccounts affected (also if either Assets, Liability, Owner's Equity, Income or Expenses)
3. **I**ncrease or **D**ecrease effect.
4. **N**ormal **B**alance of the Account (Debit or Credit)
5. **S**ide of Account should be recorded (Debit or Credit)
